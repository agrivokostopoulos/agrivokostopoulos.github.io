\section{Research Interests}

\cventry{}{Core Themes}{}{}{}{Sovereign Debt; Fiscal Sovereignty; Contract Design; Creditor Supervision; External Fiscal Control; Financial Markets; Monetary Politics; Legislative Politics; Political Institutions; State-Contingent Debt; First Age of Globalisation; Text-as-Data; LLMs; History of Economic Thought}

\cventry{}{Thesis Projects}{}{}{}{
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Sterling by Design: Contractual Standardisation and Currency Dominance on the London Stock Exchange, 1869--1929'' (draft). Uses a security-level panel of 957 sovereign debt instruments from the \textit{Investor's Monthly Manual}. Shows sterling's share of income flows rose from 79 to 99 per cent as multi-currency clauses, bullion clauses, and non-sterling denominations disappeared; Bai--Perron breaks in the late 1870s--1880s precede periphery gold adoption; captive borrowers paid a yield premium over outside-option borrowers.
\item ``The Price of Lost Fiscal Sovereignty: Greece under International Financial Control, 1898--1929'' (draft). Asks what premium or discount the market attaches to a loss of fiscal sovereignty, read in the relative prices of creditors of the same name. After the Law of Control of 1898 the senior Monopoly Loan of 1887 sometimes yields more than the junior Rentes of 1889: seniority was paid in revenue-linked coupons, with no conversion. Egypt after 1880 and the Ottoman Empire after 1881 are the comparisons (conversion into new series with a reserved senior tranche). Sources: Corporation of Foreign Bondholders, \textit{Investor's Monthly Manual}, \textit{Stock Exchange Year-Book}.
\item ``Permeable Patronage: Fiscal Crises and Domain-Specific Defection in the Greek Parliament, 1875--1905'' (draft; GRC submission, May 2026). New roll-call dataset from digitised Hellenic Parliament proceedings: 755 nominal divisions, 132,246 MP--vote observations, 1,049 matched MPs. Patronage discipline holds by default and breaks at constituency-targeting fiscal bills after the 1893 default, notably currant taxation on the coastal Peloponnese, rather than a shift from clientelism to programmatic politics.
\item ``Between Civilisation and Constitution: Greek Parliamentary Debates on Gold Standard Adoption, 1875--1885'' (draft; GRC submission, May 2026). Reconstructs 988 sittings of the Hellenic Parliament (415 monetary). Greece was on gold for eight months in 1885, entering and leaving by royal decree; convertibility was never put to a vote. The chamber had a vocabulary for forced circulation, not for the standard, and no named theorist of gold, silver, or bimetallism is cited on the metallic constitution. Asks what a legislature does with an economic idea it has no vocabulary to argue, and maps the six political registers available on the floor.
\end{itemize}}

\cventry{}{Outside Interests}{}{}{}{
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Made in Greece or Echoed from Europe? Sentiment and Awareness Among International Partners During the 1931--1932 Greek Sovereign Debt Crisis'' (draft). Employs a novel LLM-based text-as-data approach on the Tsouderos Archive (private correspondence of the Bank of Greece Governor) to map sentiment and topic trends in communications with foreign officials. Identifies two structural breaks (June and September 1931) via change point analysis; SVAR shows a significant negative relationship between archive sentiment and Greek bond yields.
\item ``Whatever Happened to Social History? Social History in the Work of the Economic History Society, 1926--2026'' (with Prof. Sir Rick Trainor, LSE). Examines the EHS's century-long relationship with social history using un-digitised Council minutes, AGM records, and sampled \textit{Economic History Review} articles and conference programmes (1927--2019). Finds thematic persistence of social topics (labour, demography, health, gender) alongside a methodological shift toward quantitative methods; the journal and conference diverged in approach while converging on the same subjects. Presented at the EHS Centenary Conference (April 2026).
\item ``Why Did Depositors Run? Withdrawals in the German Banking Crisis of 1931'' (with Philip Fliers, Queen's University Belfast). Bimonthly bank-level balance sheets (1925--1935). Severe withdrawals were widespread, highly predictable up to two years prior, and differed systematically across maturities. Withdrawals accompanied by substantial deposit reallocation across banks.
\end{itemize}}
