\cventry{}{Publications}{}{}{}{
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Made in Greece or Echoed from Europe? International Sentiment in the Tsouderos Archive and \textit{Kathimerini}, 1931--1932'' (forthcoming, \textit{Jahrbuch f\"ur Wirtschaftsgeschichte}). LLM text-as-data on 814 pages of Tsouderos's correspondence with foreign officials and 2,283 pages of \textit{Kathimerini}. International sentiment turns from May 1931 (Kreditanstalt), with breakpoints clustering around May, June, and September; the Sterling Crisis is associated with a further decline, controlling for other events and market indicators. Topic composition shifts in November--December 1931 toward debt relief and financial assistance; the domestic press moves later and more unevenly.
\end{itemize}}

\cventry{}{Thesis Projects}{}{}{}{
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Sterling by Design: Contractual Standardisation and Currency Dominance on the London Stock Exchange, 1869--1929'' (draft). Security-level panel of around 1,270 sovereign and colonial debt securities from the \textit{Investor's Monthly Manual}. Sterling's share of income flows rose from 88 to 99 per cent as multi-currency clauses, bullion clauses, and non-sterling denominations disappeared. Breaks in contract terms begin in the early 1880s, ahead of several borrowers' gold adoption, and gold status shows no detectable association with sterling denomination. Within the Government of India, sterling loans yielded about 34 bp less than matched rupee loans.
\item ``Sovereign Debt Seniority and the Reintegration of Peripheral Borrowers: Evidence from International Financial Supervision in the Ottoman Empire, Egypt, and Greece, 1870--1914'' (draft; 6th Macrohistory Conference, Bonn, September 2026). A defaulted sovereign needs new money to return, new money needs priority, and the holders already in place pay part of the cost. Under the Law of Control of 1898 the 2.5\% Guaranteed Loan entered above the whole stack, while the old gold loans were ranked in place: the senior Monopoly Loan of 1887 yielded more than the junior Rentes of 1889 in 65 of 96 months in 1900--1907, against none of 35 months before default. Egypt after 1880 and the Ottoman Empire after 1881 are the comparisons.
\item ``Permeable Patronage: Fiscal Crises and Domain-Specific Defection in the Greek Parliament, 1875--1905'' (draft; GRC submission, May 2026). New roll-call dataset from digitised Hellenic Parliament proceedings: 755 nominal divisions, 132,246 MP--vote observations, 1,049 matched MPs. Patronage discipline holds by default and breaks at constituency-targeting fiscal bills after the 1893 default: in the six core currant divisions of 1894--95, high-currant Peloponnesian deputies broke with their bloc in 31 per cent of votes, against 3 per cent for low-currant deputies. The break is domain-specific rather than a shift from clientelism to programmatic politics.
\item ``Between Civilisation and Constitution: Greek Parliamentary Debates on Gold Standard Adoption, 1875--1885'' (draft; WEast Workshop, Belgrade, August 2026). Reads 988 sittings of the Hellenic Parliament alongside the loan and bank statutes in the gazette. From 1877 forced circulation was a conditional state: the bank conventions tied its duration to the state's debt to the National and Ionian Banks and named no instrument to end it. The 1884 loan law listed the lift as a use of the proceeds without fixing a day, and Trikoupis told the chamber it would follow automatically once the money was deposited. Convertibility was in effect a term of the state's borrowing from its own banks. The chamber divided on loans, timing, and the bank conventions, but no deputy tabled a bill, amendment, or interpellation on gold, silver, or bimetallism as a regime.
\end{itemize}}

\cventry{}{Other Research}{}{}{}{
\begin{itemize}
\item ``Whatever Happened to Social History? Social History in the Work of the Economic History Society, 1926--2026'' (with Prof. Sir Rick Trainor, LSE). Examines the EHS's century-long relationship with social history using un-digitised Council minutes, AGM records, and sampled \textit{Economic History Review} articles and conference programmes (1927--2019). Finds thematic persistence of social topics (labour, demography, health, gender) alongside a methodological shift toward quantitative methods; the journal and conference diverged in approach while converging on the same subjects. Presented at the EHS Centenary Conference (April 2026).
\item ``A Predictable Crisis? Deposit Losses during the German Banking Crisis of 1931'' (with Philip Fliers, Queen's University Belfast; draft, June 2026). Bimonthly German bank balance sheets (1925--1934; 8,616 bank--month observations). Severe deposit losses were system-wide rather than confined to failed banks, highly predictable from pre-crisis funding structure as early as 1927, and differed by maturity. Aggregate contraction coexisted with predictable deposit gains at other banks.
\item ``Regional GDP in Greece, 1900--2015'' (Hellenic Observatory, with Prof. Vassilis Monastiriotis; Prof. Joan R. Ros\'es as external adviser). First NUTS-2 regional GDP estimates for Greece, built for the Ros\'es--Wolf European regional GDP database, which does not yet cover Greece. Geary--Stark estimates for 1900--1938 from the 1920 and 1930 industrial and commercial censuses, spliced to published regional figures and to ARDECO and Eurostat regional accounts from 1950; interwar agricultural output from Socrates Petmezas's prefecture-level series (University of Crete).
\end{itemize}}
